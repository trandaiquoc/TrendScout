# D5 keyword extraction and trend detection

**D5 offline gate: PASS; live validation remains open.** The local presentation connects synthetic query-source observations to the module and displays a bounded sample separately from Wikimedia results. It does not establish live provider rights or production quality.

`app.discovery` extracts bounded, deterministic 1–4 word candidates directly from English synthetic observations rather than limiting candidates to the fixed query seeds. It records source, provider, document, timestamps, scope, query evidence, content fingerprint, market evidence, parser version and canonical URL; an explicit `tee shirt` alias is the only synonym merge. Simple vocabulary rules assign fashion, home, technology, food and gaming; unmatched candidates remain unclassified. Fashion/home research priority does not filter other categories. All labels are unvalidated heuristics, not claims about US population, demand, revenue or purchase probability.

Each `(source, scope, parser version, data mode)` is evaluated separately over a three-day current window and seven-day baseline. Coverage records must identify complete comparable samples, eligible document counts and observable absence within the sample; missing, partial, zero exposure or censored data cannot prove a zero. The denominator is the sample size, not searches or people. Duplicate normalized content counts once per day while query relationships remain in evidence. Minimum support and observations on multiple days avoid treating one spike as an established trend. With sufficient coverage, zero baseline is `EMERGING` only within that sample; ratios are not calculated from zero. Classification includes accelerating, sustained, stable, declining and a cautious seasonal heuristic requiring two earlier comparable annual windows. Unknown geography in the current window cannot borrow a US label from historical evidence. Extraction and derived metrics for live data require explicit current source grants, separate from API access.

Candidate selection for future verification is bounded by server-side request budget and freshness and does not call providers or modify the existing query plan. Product suggestions are optional research hypotheses, separate from trend classification. The algorithm, taxonomy and limitations must be validated against actual permitted data and measured false positives before production; a category suggestion is not a recommendation backed by sales or intent.

Discovery records origin (`SYNTHETIC` or `LIVE`) independently from delivery (`FRESH` or `CACHED`); an assessment with cached evidence includes a limitation. A cached synthetic observation stays synthetic. Public distributed Fetch now maps retained normalized results through `app.discovery.source_results`; it exposes bounded candidate/evidence results through Fetch status and the dashboard's keyword table and detail. Every snapshot-only public candidate remains `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. Current public composition accepts synthetic handlers only; this wiring does not verify or enable a live discovery path or multi-host cache.
